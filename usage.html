Usage Guide

This guide provides an overview of how to use prop-sapt for your calculations.

Basic Workflow

The typical workflow for using prop-sapt involves:

  1. Define your dimer geometry

  2. Create a Dimer object

  3. Calculate SAPT energy decomposition

  4. Compute interaction-induced properties and/or density matrices

  5. Analyze and visualize results

Creating a Dimer

The fundamental object in prop-sapt is the Dimer class. It is created by providing a geometry string in Psi4 format, which should include two fragments (for both monomers) separated by --. Set Psi4’s output file, memory and basis sets first, as for any Psi4 calculation:

import psi4
from prop_sapt import Dimer

psi4.core.set_output_file("output.dat", False)
psi4.set_memory("4 GB")
psi4.set_options(
    {
        "basis": "aug-cc-pvdz",
        "df_basis_scf": "aug-cc-pvdz-jkfit",
        "df_basis_sapt": "aug-cc-pvdz-ri",
    }
)

# Define geometry in Psi4 format
geometry = """
0 1
O  0.000000  0.000000  0.000000
H  0.758602  0.000000  0.504284
H  0.260455  0.000000 -0.872893
--
0 1
O  3.000000  0.000000  0.000000
H  3.758602  0.000000  0.504284
H  3.260455  0.000000 -0.872893
units angstrom
"""

# Create dimer with optional parameters
dimer = Dimer(geometry, reference="RHF")

The dimer runs the monomer SCF calculations with Psi4 in the dimer-centred basis set and takes Psi4’s memory setting (Dimer(memory=...) is deprecated). The reference is "RHF" or "RKS". The dimer keeps the monomer JK objects that the CPSCF equations need itself: the Psi4 option save_jk is not needed.

If your geometry string will include more fragments, they will be treated as ghost atoms. You can specify midbonds and farbonds in this way.

The fragment structure is kept on the dimer as dimer.fragments (DimerFragments): the atoms of monomers A and B and of any extra fragments, their charges and multiplicities, the ghost atoms, and the coordinates in bohr. parse_fragments() reads the same from a geometry string or a .p4m file, without running any calculation:

from prop_sapt.fragments import parse_fragments

fragments = parse_fragments("geos/geo1.p4m")
fragments.A.atoms, fragments.B.atoms     # e.g. (0, 1, 2), (3, 4, 5)
fragments.A.charge, fragments.B.multiplicity
fragments.extra                          # fragments after the second, e.g. midbonds

SAPT Energy Calculations

To perform the SAPT energy decomposition, SAPT0 for an RHF dimer and SAPT(DFT) for an RKS one:

from prop_sapt import calc_sapt_energy

# Calculate all SAPT components
energy_result = calc_sapt_energy(dimer)

print(energy_result)

The result is a pandas Series of all SAPT energy components. It is also saved to sapt_results.csv; results="energies.csv" chooses another file and results=False saves none.

The dispersion is the uncoupled one: DISP2 and EXCH-DISP2 are built from the monomer orbitals and orbital energies, without the response of the monomers to each other.

  • With RHF, that is the dispersion of SAPT0, as in Psi4’s SAPT0.

  • With RKS, it is the uncoupled dispersion from the Kohn–Sham orbitals. Psi4’s SAPT(DFT) uses the coupled dispersion by default, so its dispersion and total energies differ from propSAPT’s. Compare with Psi4’s uncoupled dispersion instead.

Coupled dispersion is not implemented (see Known limitations). The dispersion terms of the properties and density matrices are the uncoupled ones too.

Property Calculations

To calculate interaction-induced properties for a general one-electron operator:

from prop_sapt import calc_property
import numpy as np

# A one-electron operator in the dimer's AO basis, an (nbf, nbf) array
property_matrix = np.array([...])

# Calculate property contributions
property_result = calc_property(dimer, property_matrix)

print(property_result)

For predefined properties, you can use the convenience functions, e.g., to calculate the dipole moment:

from prop_sapt import calc_property

dipole_result = calc_property(dimer, "dipole")

The property is the field derivative of the SAPT energy, and like calc_sapt_energy() it takes response, which chooses the induction amplitudes of the second-order induction terms:

uncoupled = calc_property(dimer, "dipole", response=False)

the coupled terms

The monomers relax in the field in every mode, so the first-order, dispersion and exchange-dispersion terms do not depend on response. With response=False no CPSCF equations are solved for the induction amplitudes or their field derivatives; the two left are the responses of the monomers to the property operator. This helps when comparing methods and when isolating problems in the CPSCF solver.

The S^inf exchange-dispersion term x2_exch-disp, and with it the total x_induced, has a known error; see Known limitations.

Density Matrix Calculations

To compute interaction-induced density matrix components from propSAPT:

from prop_sapt import calc_densities

density_matrices = calc_densities(dimer)

The result is a dictionary of density matrices: "<component>_A", "<component>_B" and their sum "<component>" for each component of DENSITY_COMPONENTS ("pol", "exch", "ind", …). The matrices are for one spin, the Da convention: the total density is twice the matrix.

The second-order exchange components of each monomer need theta amplitudes of both monomers. calc_density_matrix() computes them once and keeps them on the dimer for the other monomer’s call, and calc_densities() frees them when both monomers are done. If you call calc_density_matrix() for each monomer yourself, free them afterwards with clear_theta_amplitudes(): the exchange-dispersion ones take as much memory as the dispersion amplitudes.

Most of the time goes into solving CPSCF equations, one for each component of each monomer and for the theta amplitudes. calc_density_matrix() solves the equations of a monomer together, in one run of the solver (Dimer.cpscf_batch). Each iteration then builds the J and K matrices of all of them at once, which costs much less than solving them one by one.

Those J and K matrices come from the monomer SCF’s J/K object, so they follow SCF_TYPE: with DIRECT, every iteration recomputes the four-index integrals. The cpscf_type option switches the CPSCF solves of the densities and the properties to density fitting, while the SAPT energies keep the monomer SCF’s J/K object and stay consistent with Psi4’s SAPT:

from prop_sapt import set_propsapt_options

set_propsapt_options({"cpscf_type": "DF", "df_basis_cpscf": "aug-cc-pvqz-jkfit"})

cpscf_type is None (the default, the monomer SCF’s J/K object), "DIRECT" or "DF". df_basis_cpscf is the fitting basis of "DF"; it defaults to DF_BASIS_SCF as it was when the dimer was built, or else the JKFIT basis of the dimer’s orbital basis. With "DF" the densities and properties solve their own induction amplitudes and differ from the DIRECT ones by a small fitting error, which a larger fitting basis such as aug-cc-pVQZ-JKFIT lowers further. The gain is largest with SCF_TYPE DIRECT and HF, where the density matrices become several times faster. With DFT the exchange-correlation kernel takes a large part of the CPSCF time, so the gain is smaller. In a run configuration, set the options under propsapt_options.

Every matrix is symmetric. calc_density_matrix() and save_densities() check this and warn with InvariantWarning if a matrix is not symmetric within the check_symmetry_atol option (1e-10; None turns the check off). Bundles written by 0.4.0.dev0 before 2026-10-14 hold second-order exchange components (exch-ind_S2, exch-ind, exch-disp_S2 and the Ind, Disp and total sums) with an antisymmetric part. It does not enter densities, cubes or traces with symmetric operators, but symmetrize such matrices with 0.5 * (D + D.T) before an eigendecomposition (e.g. natural orbitals) or before writing one triangle of them (e.g. into an fchk file).

With save_cubes=True the total densities are saved as .cube files for visualization. With save_matrices=True the matrices are saved, always in the AO basis, into one self-describing bundle, densities.npz. It also holds the monomer wavefunctions and the metadata: the geometry and its fragments, the basis set and the fitting bases, the method, the Psi4 and propSAPT options, the versions, and the conventions. The basis sets and the Psi4 options are those the dimer was built with. Nothing in it is pickled. Read it back with load_densities(), which checks the conventions and, if asked, that the matrices belong to a geometry, a dimer or a basis set:

from prop_sapt import load_densities

densities = load_densities("results/geo1", geometry="geos/geo1.p4m")
rho = densities.matrices["total_A"]      # one spin, AO basis
rho_total = densities.total("total_A")   # both spins, 2 * rho
densities.fragments.A.atoms              # fragment structure
densities.metadata["basis"]["blend"]     # e.g. "AUG-CC-PVTZ"
wfn_A = densities.wavefunction("A")      # psi4.core.Wavefunction of monomer A

A bundle that does not match raises ArtifactError. save_densities() writes a bundle of any AO matrices of a dimer. The older layout of bare delta_dm_<key>.npy and wfn_mon_<A/B>.npy files is still written with matrix_format="npy", but it is deprecated, and so is the compress_matrices argument.

Finite-Field Properties

Two wrappers compute interaction-induced dipoles numerically, as field derivatives of energies. Each places the system in a uniform electric field of ±field_strength along every axis (6 fields for the two-point formula, 12 for the four-point one) through Psi4’s PERTURB_H, and differentiates the energies. Both take the geometry string, not a Dimer, and use the Psi4 options set at the time. Only "dipole" is available.

SAPT

finite_field_sapt() differentiates every component of calc_sapt_energy(), building a dimer for each field. The result has one row per axis and one column per energy component (ELST1, EXCH1, IND2, DISP2, …). Each column is the finite-field counterpart of a calc_property() term, so the analytic properties can be checked term by term; examples/finite_field_sapt.py lines them up:

from prop_sapt import finite_field_sapt

ff_dipole = finite_field_sapt(
    geometry,
    "dipole",
    field_strength=0.001,
    formula="two_point",     # or "four_point"
)

sapt_kwargs is passed on to calc_sapt_energy(), e.g. sapt_kwargs={"response": False}. The result is also saved to results-ff.csv (results= chooses another file), and the SAPT energies of every field to results-<axis>-<sign><field>.csv, both in the working directory.

Any Psi4 energy method

finite_field_property() does the same for any method psi4.energy runs: "scf", a density functional, "mp2", "ccsd", and so on. With cp_correction=True each energy is the counterpoise-corrected interaction energy (bsse_type="cp"). The result is then the supermolecular interaction-induced dipole of that method, a reference for the SAPT values:

from prop_sapt.finite_field import finite_field_property

ff_ccsd = finite_field_property(
    geometry,
    "ccsd",
    "dipole",
    cp_correction=True,
    field_strength=0.001,
    formula="four_point",
)

The derivative includes the orbital relaxation. For the HF molecule, the finite-field DF-MP2 and CCSD dipoles agree with Psi4’s analytic orbital-relaxed ones within 3e-9 a.u. Without cp_correction the total energy of the system is differentiated. Psi4’s field acts on the electrons only, so that gives the electronic part of the dipole, which depends on the origin; the nuclear parts cancel in an interaction energy. The result is also saved to results-ff-<method>.csv (results= chooses another file).

Command-Line Runner

For production runs, the prop-sapt command runs a calculation from a configuration file, with no Python script needed:

prop-sapt run config.yaml geos/geo1.p4m --memory 32 --threads 8

The configuration (YAML or JSON) sets the reference, basis sets, resources, Psi4 and propSAPT options, the output directory and the tasks to run. See examples/run_config.yaml for a commented example:

reference: RHF
basis: aug-cc-pvtz
df_basis: aug-cc-pvtz     # DF_BASIS_SCF = aug-cc-pvtz-jkfit, DF_BASIS_SAPT = aug-cc-pvtz-ri
memory: 2                 # GB
threads: 2
psi4_options:
  scf_type: direct
  e_convergence: 1.0e-10
output:
  dir: "{geometry}"       # expands to the geometry file name without its extension
tasks:
  monomer_references: true   # monomer density cubes and .fchk files
  sapt_energy: true          # sapt_results.csv
  densities: true            # densities.npz and delta_dm_*.cube

Values come from the built-in defaults, then the file, then the command-line flags (--memory, --threads, --reference, --functional, --grac-a, --grac-b, --basis, --df-basis, --output-dir). The available tasks are monomer_references, sapt_energy, property and densities; they always run in that order. The fitting bases can also be given by their full names (df_basis_scf, df_basis_sapt). Without df_basis or df_basis_scf, DF_BASIS_SCF is left to Psi4’s default.

Elements that lack a basis set can be given a substitute with basis_overrides, either inline or as a JSON file next to the configuration:

{"Zn": {"DF_BASIS_SCF": "def2-tzvpd-ri", "DF_BASIS_SAPT": "def2-tzvpd-ri"}}

Add --dry-run to print the resolved configuration and Psi4 options without calculating anything. Each run also writes propsapt_run.json into the output directory, recording the resolved configuration, the Psi4 options, the package versions and the fragments of the geometry. The densities task saves its matrices into densities.npz (matrix_format: npz, the default), whose metadata also holds the run configuration and the basis overrides applied; matrix_format: npy (deprecated) writes bare .npy files. The command exits with status 0 on success, 1 if a calculation failed and 2 for an invalid configuration, so it can be used directly in batch scripts, e.g. in a SLURM array job:

GEO_FILES=(geos/*.p4m)
prop-sapt run config.yaml "${GEO_FILES[$SLURM_ARRAY_TASK_ID]}" \
    --memory "$MEMORY" --threads "$SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK"

Basis sets missing from Psi4

Basis sets that Psi4’s library lacks, for some or all elements, can come from the Basis Set Exchange (BSE) or from ccRepo, which also holds correlation-consistent fitting sets (_JKFIT, _MP2FIT) for elements missing them in Psi4. There are two ways to use them.

For the whole molecule, from the BSE: Psi4 reads names prefixed with bse: from the basis_set_exchange package (pip install basis_set_exchange), e.g. df_basis_scf: bse:def2-universal-jkfit. Such names cannot be used in basis_overrides, and df_basis appends -jkfit/-ri, which rarely match BSE names, so give df_basis_scf/df_basis_sapt in full.

As files, from either source: prop-sapt basis fetch writes a basis set into a Psi4-format file once, and runs then read it through basis_dirs, with no network or extra packages needed on the compute nodes:

prop-sapt basis fetch ccrepo aug-cc-pVTZ_MP2FIT --elements Zn --dir basis
prop-sapt basis fetch bse <basis set> --elements <elements> --dir basis

Each file is named after its basis set, <source>-<basis set> by default (--as chooses another name), e.g. basis/ccrepo-aug-cc-pvtz_mp2fit.gbs holds the basis set ccrepo-aug-cc-pVTZ_MP2FIT, and starts with a record of its source, version and date. A file holding a few elements is used for those elements through basis_overrides, while the others keep their default. For example, for Zn with aug-cc-pVTZ, whose fitting sets Psi4 lacks, the RI set can come from ccRepo and the JKFIT set from Psi4’s library:

df_basis: aug-cc-pvtz
basis_dirs: [basis]       # relative to the configuration file
basis_overrides:
  Zn: {DF_BASIS_SCF: def2-universal-jkfit, DF_BASIS_SAPT: ccrepo-aug-cc-pVTZ_MP2FIT}

A file must not take the name of a basis set in Psi4’s library: Psi4 reads the first file of a name it finds, so a file holding a few elements would hide the library basis set for every other element. prop-sapt basis fetch refuses such names, refuses to overwrite a file without --force, and writes nothing if the basis set is missing for a requested element.

The sources are used through optional packages, installed separately and only where prop-sapt basis fetch runs; propSAPT itself does not depend on them:

  • bse: pip install basis_set_exchange (or conda install -c conda-forge basis_set_exchange);

  • ccrepo: pip install git+https://github.com/Sheffield-Theoretical-Chemistry/ccrepo_utility colorlog tqdm requests (ccrepo_utility does not install its own dependencies, and it is licensed for non-commercial use only).

Please cite the source and the original publications of the basis sets you use.

The same runner is available from Python:

from prop_sapt import run

results = run("config.yaml", "geos/geo1.p4m")
sapt_energy = results["sapt_energy"]

python -m prop_sapt run ... is equivalent to prop-sapt run ....

Advanced Options

Customizing Calculations

You can customize various aspects of the calculation using Psi4 options before creating the Dimer object and running calculations:

psi4.set_options(
    {
        "basis": "aug-cc-pvtz",
        "DF_BASIS_SCF": "aug-cc-pvtz-jkfit",
        "DF_BASIS_SAPT": "aug-cc-pvtz-ri",
        "scf_type": "direct",
        "e_convergence": 1e-12,
        "d_convergence": 1e-12,
        # Integral thresholds for higher precision
        "ints_tolerance": 1e-14,
        "screening": "schwarz",
        "cholesky_tolerance": 1e-10,
    }
)

Hellmann-Feynman Forces

To compute Hellmann-Feynman forces on nuclei from one-particle AO density matrices:

from prop_sapt.forces import calc_force_at_nuc, calc_force_nuc_nuc

mol = dimer.get_psi4_molecule()
basisset = dimer.get_psi4_basisset()

# Force on nucleus 0 from the interaction-induced density of monomer A
force = calc_force_at_nuc(mol, 2 * rho_A, atidx=0, basisset=basisset, add_nuclear=False)

# Pass multiple density matrices — integrals are computed only once
forces = calc_force_at_nuc(
    mol, [2 * rho_A_elst, 2 * rho_A_exch], atidx=0, basisset=basisset, add_nuclear=False
)

# Nuclear-nuclear repulsion only (no electron density needed)
force_nuc = calc_force_nuc_nuc(mol, atidx=0)

All forces are in atomic units (Hartree/Bohr). The nuclear-nuclear repulsion contribution is added by default; pass add_nuclear=False to obtain the electronic part only, as for interaction-induced densities above. The matrices must be in the AO basis of basisset; without it, a basis set is built for mol from the current BASIS option, so pass the dimer’s for its density matrices. As elsewhere, no spin factor is applied: pass 2 * rho for the matrices of calc_densities().

Evaluating at Arbitrary Points

The prop_sapt.evaluate module evaluates densities, orbitals and the electrostatic potential (ESP) of AO matrices at any set of points: a quadrature grid, a line, a plane. Points are in bohr, as an array of shape (..., 3), and the result has the shape of the points without the last axis.

import numpy as np
from prop_sapt.evaluate import evaluate_density, evaluate_orbital, evaluate_esp

basisset = dimer.get_psi4_basisset()

# density along the x axis
line = np.zeros((200, 3))
line[:, 0] = np.linspace(-6.0, 6.0, 200)
rho = evaluate_density(rho_A, line, basisset)                 # shape (200,)

# several matrices in one call share the basis function values
rhos = evaluate_density([rho_elst, rho_exch], line, basisset)  # shape (2, 200)

# orbitals are the columns of the MO coefficients, as in Psi4's Ca()
C = dimer.wfnA.Ca().np
psi = evaluate_orbital(C[:, :3], line, basisset)              # shape (3, 200)

# ESP of the electrons; nuclei=True adds the nuclei of the basis set's molecule
esp = evaluate_esp(rho_A, line, basisset)

The same functions are Dimer methods that use the dimer basis set: dimer.evaluate_density(rho, points), dimer.evaluate_orbital(C, points) and dimer.evaluate_esp(rho, points).

  • Matrices must be in the AO basis of basisset, in Psi4 AO order.

  • No spin factor is applied. The density matrices returned by calc_densities() follow the Da convention, so pass 2 * rho for the total (RHF) density, as the density cubes do.

  • Basis function values are computed for blocks of chunk_size points, which takes about chunk_size * nbf floats of memory. The default is the point_eval_chunk_size option (2000).

Integrals over space become weighted sums on a quadrature grid, for example Psi4’s molecular grid from prop_sapt.partition.molecular_grid():

from prop_sapt.partition import molecular_grid

grid = molecular_grid(basisset)
n_elec = np.sum(grid.weights * evaluate_density(2 * rho_A, grid.points, basisset))

Fragment Populations

The prop_sapt.partition module turns a partition of space between fragments into matrices, computed once per geometry. With fragment weights \(w_X(\mathbf{r})\) on a quadrature grid, the weighted-overlap matrix of fragment \(X\) is

\[W^X_{\mu\nu} = \int w_X(\mathbf{r})\, \phi_\mu(\mathbf{r})\, \phi_\nu(\mathbf{r})\, d\mathbf{r},\]

and the electrons any density matrix \(D\) puts on \(X\) are the trace \(\mathrm{Tr}(D W^X)\), with no further grid work. The first-moment matrices (\(\mathbf{r}\) times the same integrand) give fragment dipoles the same way.

ops = dimer.fragment_operators("hirshfeld")   # or "becke"

q_A = ops.charge("A", 2 * rho_pol)            # charge moved onto A, -Tr(D W_A)
n_B = ops.population("B", 2 * rho_A)          # electrons on B, Tr(D W_B)
q_all = ops.charge("A", [2 * rho_elst, 2 * rho_exch])     # one value per matrix
mu_A = ops.dipole("A", 2 * rho_pol, origin=com_A)         # electronic dipole of A

  • "becke" assigns each point to the Becke cell of an atom of monomer A or B.

  • "hirshfeld" uses \(w_X = \rho^0_X / (\rho^0_A + \rho^0_B)\) from the unperturbed monomer densities.

  • The grid is Psi4’s molecular quadrature grid, 75 radial times 590 Lebedev points per atom by default (options quadrature_radial_points, quadrature_spherical_points). Its radial extent is twice Psi4’s DFT default (option quadrature_bs_radius_alpha), which diffuse basis sets need: the operators of fragments that cover the whole system sum to the overlap matrix within about 1e-6 for aug-cc-pVTZ.

  • As in prop_sapt.evaluate, no spin factor is applied: pass 2 * rho for the matrices of calc_densities(). Dipoles are in atomic units, with the electrons contributing negatively, as in Psi4’s ao_dipole.

Fragment weights from any other partitioning (e.g. LISA or MBIS weights from horton_part) work as well: give them at the points of the grid they were computed on, with that grid’s quadrature weights. Only these arrays are needed; the basis functions are evaluated by Psi4 in the given basis set:

from prop_sapt.partition import FragmentOperators

# points (n, 3) in bohr, quadrature weights (n,), fragment weights w_A, w_B (n,)
ops = FragmentOperators.from_grid(
    points, weights, {"A": w_A, "B": w_B}, dimer.get_psi4_basisset()
)
q_A = ops.charge("A", 2 * rho_pol)

The accuracy is then that of the given grid. By default (partition_of_unity=True) the fragments are taken to cover the whole system, and two checks guard it; like the other checks of prop_sapt.checks they warn with InvariantWarning and never stop a calculation:

  • from_grid() checks that the fragment weights sum to 1 wherever the basis functions reach: the largest element of \(\sum_q w_q (\sum_X w_X(\mathbf{r}_q) - 1)\, \phi_\mu \phi_\nu\) must be within the check_partition_atol option (1e-8). Points far from the molecule, where e.g. LISA weights stop summing to 1, do not count.

  • Every population and charge call checks that the fragments account for all the electrons of each density, \(\sum_X \mathrm{Tr}(D W^X) = \mathrm{Tr}(D S)\), within the check_closure_atol option (1e-6 electrons). The default grid closes within 1e-8 electrons for the total density of the water dimer in aug-cc-pVTZ and within 1e-9 for its interaction-induced densities.

ops.closure_error(2 * rho) gives the electrons a grid misses for a density. For fragments that do not cover the whole system, e.g. a single atom or a grid over part of space, their weights do not sum to 1: pass partition_of_unity=False (also to dimer.fragment_operators), and nothing is checked then.

Cube File Utilities

Generating cube files

Dimer.make_cube evaluates matrices in the dimer’s basis set on a Cartesian grid, with the functions of prop_sapt.evaluate. It supports three matrix types:

  • "density" — electron density from a one-particle AO density matrix

  • "orbital" — orbital amplitude from a coefficient vector

  • "esp" — electrostatic potential (AO density matrix contracted with the Coulomb kernel)

# Single density cube
cube = dimer.make_cube(2 * rho_A, obj_type="density")
cube.save("delta_dm_A.cube")

# Multiple matrices in one call (basis function values computed once)
cubes = dimer.make_cube(
    [2 * rho_elst, 2 * rho_exch, orbital_vec],
    obj_type=["density", "density", "orbital"],
)

# A finer grid than the default (cube_grid_step 0.2 bohr, cube_grid_overage 4.0 bohr)
cube = dimer.make_cube(2 * rho_B, grid_step=0.1)

prop_sapt.cubes.make_cube() does the same for any Psi4 molecule; pass the basis set the matrices are in as basisset=, e.g. dimer.get_psi4_basisset(), since without it a basis set is built from the current BASIS option. A grid from prop_sapt.cubes.prepare_grid() can be reused across calls with grid=.

Working with cubes

A prop_sapt.cubes.Cube holds values on a regular grid, in bohr. Cubes on the same grid combine with + and -, and scale with * and / by a number. The grids are checked first, and the result takes the atoms of the left cube and empty comments:

import numpy as np
from prop_sapt.cubes import Cube

rho = Cube.from_file("cubes/delta_dm_total_A.cube")
rho_pol = Cube.from_file("cubes/delta_dm_pol_A.cube")

rho_rest = rho - rho_pol
rho_rest.comment1 = "total minus polarization density of monomer A"
rho_rest.save("delta_dm_rest_A.cube")

Cube.integrate sums the values over the voxels times Cube.voxel_volume. It takes an optional weight at every voxel, either a cube on the same grid or an array of the grid’s shape. Leading axes of the array stack several weights:

rho.integrate()                       # electrons in the box
rho.integrate(w_A)                    # electrons that the weight cube w_A gives to A
rho.integrate(np.stack([w_A.volumetric_data, w_B.volumetric_data]))  # both at once
np.sqrt(rho.integrate(rho.volumetric_data))  # L2 norm
rho.moved_charge()                    # half the integral of |rho|

Integrals on a cube carry the error of its voxel grid and box. Fragment populations from the density matrices avoid it.

read_cube_file (also Cube.from_file) accepts any number of values per line. Cubes of several orbitals, with several values per voxel or in angstrom are not supported.

Known limitations

  • S^inf exchange-dispersion property. The x2_exch-disp term of calc_property() is not yet the field derivative of the S^inf exchange-dispersion energy EXCH-DISP2. For the dipole of He–H2 in aug-cc-pVTZ it is 7.2e-5 a.u. off the finite-field derivative, about 18% of the term, and the total x_induced carries that error. Every term of x_induced_S2, the total with the S^2 exchange terms, agrees with its finite-field derivative. Use it, or check a system with finite_field_sapt(). Tracked in issue #1.

  • S^2 exchange in the density totals. The second-order totals of calc_densities() use the S^2 exchange densities: Ind is ind plus exch-ind_S2, Disp is disp plus exch-disp_S2, and total sums pol, exch, Ind and Disp. The S^inf exchange-induction density exch-ind is returned on its own; an S^inf exchange-dispersion density is not implemented yet.

  • Uncoupled dispersion only. The dispersion and exchange-dispersion energies, and the dispersion terms of the properties and density matrices, are the uncoupled ones. With RHF this is the SAPT0 dispersion. With RKS it is not the coupled dispersion that Psi4’s SAPT(DFT) uses by default, so SAPT(DFT) dispersion and total energies differ from Psi4’s. Coupled dispersion is not implemented yet.

  • Closed-shell references only. The monomers must be closed-shell singlets with an RHF or RKS reference; unrestricted references are not implemented.

  • All-electron only. Frozen core is not supported, and Dimer rejects the FREEZE_CORE option.

  • Finite-field files. finite_field_sapt() writes the SAPT energies of every field into the working directory, results-<axis>-<sign><field>.csv.
